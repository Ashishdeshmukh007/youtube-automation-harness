# indian-wisdom-research

How to find and attribute the wisdom a Chariot of the Self video stands on.

## The canon (and what each is good for)

- **Bhagavad Gita** — action without attachment to results (2.47), duty/svadharma
  (3.35), steadiness of mind (2.55–58), the anatomy of anger (2.62–63).
- **Upanishads** — the Self and the witness, the nature of mind. *Katha* (the chariot;
  Nachiketa & death), *Mundaka* (two birds), *Chandogya* ("tat tvam asi").
- **Yoga Sutras (Patanjali)** — the mechanics of attention: *citta vritti nirodha*
  (1.2), the five kleshas (2.3), *sthira sukham asanam* (2.46), practice & non-
  attachment (1.12–16).
- **Chanakya (Arthashastra / Chanakya Niti)** — strategy, discipline, money, people,
  when to act and when to wait. Practical, worldly, sharp.

## Sourcing discipline (non-negotiable)

- **Verify before you cite.** Name the text and, where possible, chapter/verse. Never
  invent or paraphrase a verse into a quote. If you can't confirm attribution, say so
  or cut it.
- **Plain meaning over jargon.** Capture what the verse actually says in plain words;
  the scriptwriter needs that, not Sanskrit alone.
- **Honest parallels only.** Never claim the texts "predicted" modern science or
  psychology. Draw careful, defensible analogies.
- **Not religion.** Treat the texts as a thoughtful modern reader treats any profound
  source — wisdom, not worship. Never frame another tradition as wrong.

## From text to topic

A usable topic = **one modern problem** + **one specific idea/verse** + **one surprising
angle** + **roughly the practice** it implies + **a working title**. Cross-check
`topics/backlog.md` and `episodes/` so you don't repeat a recent topic.
