# The dharma you're skipping

Shot: a phone screen glowing in a dark bedroom, late evening, soft warm light spilling onto rumpled sheets, no face, no text visible, contemplative mood, muted earth tones
Sfx: phone-ring

It's late at night. You're on your phone.

A friend just posted about a promotion.
An old classmate is suddenly engaged.
Someone you barely know is sending back photos from somewhere that doesn't look like your life.

You don't resent them. You just feel... behind.

You close the app. You open another one. You do the same thing again in twenty minutes.
You go to bed a little later than you meant to, and you don't sleep quite as well, and the next morning the world feels slightly smaller than it did the day before.

[beat]

Shot: a single person standing on a darkened street, looking up at the lit windows of an apartment building, low angle, the figure small against the many windows, warm interior light contrasting with the cool night air outside

And the worst part isn't even the envy.
It's the small, quiet shame that comes after.
The feeling that you should be doing more. That everyone else figured out some trick you missed. That you're late to a party that started without you.

Sound familiar?

[beat]

Shot: Arjuna kneeling on the wooden floor of a great chariot, head bowed, his bow lying untouched across his knees, dawn light just touching the horizon behind him, dust settling in the still air around his shoulders

Here's something strange.

Two thousand three hundred years ago, on the edge of a battlefield, a man collapsed.
His name was Arjuna.
He was the best archer in the world, and he could not lift his bow.

[beat]

Shot: an ancient battlefield at dawn, long banners still, the dust settled, no people in the foreground, only the suggestion of two camps in the far distance, soft golden haze

He looked out across that field at the lives he was supposed to be living — the duty of a warrior, the expectations of his family, the path that everyone agreed was his path.
And something in him went, "I can't."

It's not a war story, though.
It's a feeling story.

The feeling is: I see all the paths I'm supposed to walk. I see what they want from me. I see what I appear to be from the outside. And none of it matches what I actually want to do.
And that gap is unbearable.

You have felt this. Maybe this week.

[beat]

Shot: Krishna seated beside Arjuna on the chariot, one hand resting on his own knee, looking out at the horizon rather than at Arjuna, calm and unhurried, the dawn light soft on his face

A friend sat beside Arjuna on that chariot.
His name was Krishna.
He didn't lecture. He didn't even look at Arjuna at first.
He just sat there, unhurried, and after a while he said a few things.

One of them has stayed with me for years.

[beat]

Shot: a still pool of water at dawn, perfectly reflecting a single ancient tree, no ripples, no wind, the reflection so clear it looks like another world underneath, soft cool palette with one warm note in the sky

He said: "Better is one's own dharma, though imperfectly performed, than the dharma of another, well performed."

The Gita, chapter three, verse thirty-five.

Dharma. The word gets translated many ways. Duty. Calling. Path. Nature. Law.
For this moment, let's use the simplest one: the thing that is yours to do.

Not the right thing in general.
The specific thing. Yours. This season. With your hands, your limits, your one short life.

[beat]

Shot: a single set of footprints in soft sand at sunrise, stretching into the distance, no other prints, just one path, calm and clear, warm light, no horizon visible — the path itself is the world

Here's what I think that verse is really saying.

Comparison breaks because it treats everyone's dharma as if it were the same currency.

The friend who got promoted isn't writing the book they someday want to write — they're being promoted. They've skipped their own path for a season, in order to do this other thing.
The couple posting from Bali isn't building the quiet project they keep telling their friends about — they're on holiday. They've set something down.

Every single person you envy has a different thing they set down, for the moment, to do the thing you're looking at.

You are looking at their highlight reel.
They are not, in that moment, doing their dharma either.

You're measuring yourself against a different movie.

[beat]

Shot: a hand resting on a closed notebook on a wooden table, late afternoon light falling across the page, the hand at rest not writing, the moment of pause before beginning, soft warm palette, intimate and still

There is a strange relief in this.

You are not behind.
You are on your road.

The Bali poster is on theirs.
The promoted friend is on theirs.
You are on yours.

The trap isn't that other people are ahead. The trap is that you let the looking replace the walking.
The trap is that the sideways glance becomes your evening. Becomes your Sunday morning. Becomes, slowly, the inner weather of your week.

[beat]

So. What do you do with this?

I want to give you one thing to try. Not forever. Not as a new identity. Just for this week.

Sit down somewhere quiet — at the start of your week is best, Sunday night or Monday morning. A kitchen table. A bench somewhere. Anywhere you can think for five minutes without your phone.

Take a piece of paper. Or open a notes file. Anywhere you'll see it again.

Take a breath first. Don't rush this. The page is doing something for you that's hard to see but easy to feel.

Write down one task for this season.

[beat]

Shot: a hand writing in a notebook with a fountain pen, close-up, soft natural light from a window, the handwriting visible but not the words, intimate and personal

Not the year. The season — the next three months.
Not your ten goals. One.
Not the polished version. The real one.

It can be small. "Finish the draft." "Have the conversation I've been avoiding." "Get to a place where my back doesn't hurt every morning." "Learn enough cooking that I stop eating out of containers." "Stop drinking on weekdays for a month." "Send the email."

It just has to be yours. Not your mother's. Not the algorithm's. Not the version of you that exists on LinkedIn.

[beat]

Then — and this is the part that does the work — measure only against that.

When you catch yourself looking sideways, at their job, their house, their relationship, their follower count, their Sunday morning…
You bring your eyes back to that page.
That is the only scoreboard that belongs to you.

If you don't write it down, the sideways glance will pull you off your path every single time.
The page is what makes the path real.

A good day stops being "I scrolled less than yesterday." It becomes "I moved one inch closer to my page."
That's a different life. Even when the inch is small.

One page. One task. One season. Then again.

[beat]

Shot: a chariot wheel half-buried in soft earth at sunset, the wheel still, no chariot, no horses, only the relic of a journey that has paused, warm dusk light, contemplative and quiet

A small thing, before we close.

The Upanishads use the image of the chariot for the self.
The rider is who you really are.
The body is the chariot.
The mind is the reins.

The reins go slack when you look sideways.
There's no hand on them.
Just a horse, running toward someone else's field.

The charioteer is the part of you that knows:
This is my road.
Stay on it.

One page. One task. One season. Stay on your road.

[end]
