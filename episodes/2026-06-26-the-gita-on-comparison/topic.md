# The dharma you're skipping

## The problem (in the viewer's words)

You open Instagram. A friend just got promoted. An old classmate just bought
a flat. Someone you barely know is posting from a beach in Bali. And you're
sitting at your kitchen table at 11pm, slightly heavier than when you sat
down.

You don't resent them. You just feel... behind.

The worst part is the math: if they're ahead, and they're doing what you
should be doing, then whatever you're doing must be wrong. So you close the
app, open something else, and do the same thing again in twenty minutes.

## The source

**Bhagavad Gita 3.35** — Krishna to Arjuna, on doing your own work:

> *śreyān sva-dharmo viguṇaḥ para-dharmāt sv-anuṣṭhitāt*
> *sva-dharme nidhanaṁ śreyaḥ para-dharmo bhayāvahaḥ*

"Better is one's own dharma, though imperfectly performed, than the dharma
of another, well performed. Better is death in one's own dharma; the dharma
of another is fraught with danger."

(Easwaran translation, public domain.)

## The angle (the one surprising connection)

The word the Gita uses here — *dharma* — gets translated many ways: duty,
calling, nature, path. For this video, the cleanest translation is **the
thing that is yours to do**. Not "the right thing in general." The
specific thing that fits *you*, in *this* season, with *your* skills and
limitations.

Comparison breaks because it treats everyone's dharma as if they were the
same currency. The Bali poster isn't getting promoted — they're posting
from Bali. Your friend who got promoted isn't writing the book they
someday want to write — they're being promoted. Each of them has skipped
their own dharma for a season to do something else. So have you.

The verse isn't saying "stop looking at other people." It's saying: **what
you see is not the same thing as what they have**. You're measuring
yourself against the highlight reel of a *different movie*.

## The practice (what the viewer can do this week)

Write down **one task for this season**. Not the year. The season —
the next ~3 months. Something specific to your work, your craft, your
relationships, your health.

Then **measure only against that**. When you catch yourself looking
sideways — at their job, their house, their relationship, their follower
count — bring your eyes back to the page where you wrote that one task.
That's the only scoreboard that belongs to you.

If you didn't write it down, the sideways glance will pull you off your
path every single time.

## The chariot (the closing resonance)

The reins go slack when you look sideways — there's no hand on them, just
a horse running toward someone else's field. The charioteer is the part
of you that knows: this is my road. Stay on it.
